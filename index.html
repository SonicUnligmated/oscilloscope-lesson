What is an oscilloscope?

An oscilloscope (CRO) draws a live graph of an electrical signal. Vertical = voltage. Horizontal = time. The bright line — the trace — is the waveform.

In this lab a function generator feeds triangular, square, and sinusoidal waves into the CRO. The same skill — reading amplitude and timing from a voltage–time display — underlies ECG, EEG, and EMG monitoring.

Formulas & results

1. Amplitude (A)

Peak-to-peak from the vertical scale:

$$V_{PP} = Y\text{ deflection} \times \frac{\text{volts}}{\text{div}}$$

Amplitude (half of peak-to-peak):

$$A = \frac{V_{PP}}{2}\ \text{volt}$$
2. Time period (T)

Duration of one complete cycle:

$$T = X\text{ deflection} \times \frac{\text{Time}}{\text{div}}$$
3. Frequency (f)
$$f = \frac{1}{T}\ \text{Hz}$$

Put $T$ in seconds before taking $1/T$: 1 ms $= 10^{-3}$ s.

Worked result (from the graded lab)

Scale on the CRO: volts/div $= 0.5$ V/div · time/div $= 0.2$ ms/div · $Y = 3.7$ div · $X = 3.3$ div.

  1. Peak-to-peak$V_{PP} = 3.7 \times 0.5 = 1.85\ \text{V}$
  2. Amplitude$A = 1.85 \div 2 = 0.925\ \text{V}$
  3. Period$T = 3.3 \times 0.2 = 0.66\ \text{ms}$
  4. Convert to s$T = 0.66\ \text{ms} = 0.66 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{s} = 6.6 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{s}$
  5. Frequency$f = \dfrac{1}{6.6 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{s}} = 1515.15\ \text{Hz} \approx 1515\ \text{Hz}$

Common slip: $1 / 0.66 = 1.51$ Hz is wrong. That divides by milliseconds. Convert $T$ to seconds first.

  • Amplitude of the given waveform $A =$ 0.925 volt
  • Time period of the given waveform $T =$ 6.6 × 10⁻⁴ second (= 0.66 ms)
  • Frequency of the given waveform $f =$ 1515.15 hertz
